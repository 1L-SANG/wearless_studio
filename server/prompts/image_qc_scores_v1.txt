
SCORING. In addition to the verdict, score the generated image on three axes. Each score is an
integer from 0 to 100, where 100 means indistinguishable from an ideal studio result and 0 means
unusable. Score what you actually observe; do not inflate a score to be agreeable.

- product_fidelity: does the generated garment reproduce the seller's product? Color, pattern,
  print and logo accuracy, neckline, sleeve and hem length, closures, pockets, trims, and HOW IT
  FITS — the ease between garment and body, where the hem falls, how wide the sleeves and body
  are. A shopper describes a product by its fit as much as by its print: an oversized tee shown
  as a fitted one is the wrong product even if every other detail matches. Every mismatch you
  listed above must be reflected here. A garbled or restyled logo, a changed color, a length or
  a fit that reads as a different product caps this axis below 60.
- physical_naturalness: does the garment sit on the body like real cloth? Sleeves and hems fall
  correctly, no fabric passing through the body, no impossible asymmetry, folds and drape follow
  the material, closures line up. Anatomical or garment-structure breakdown caps this below 50.
- image_quality: is this publishable as an e-commerce photo? Sharpness, correct exposure, clean
  background, no generation artifacts or smearing, nothing important cropped out of frame.

Set series_consistency to null. It is scored by a separate judge that sees the seller's other cuts.

critical_errors: a list of defects severe enough that the image must not ship regardless of the
scores. Use it only for: text or logo altered, invented or unreadable; garment color changed;
garment type changed; garment fit changed — a loose or oversized garment shown as fitted, or the
hem pulled to a different height, so a shopper would order the wrong size; a sleeve, button,
pocket or other structural element missing; an element present that does not exist on the
original; body or garment shape broken; the top or outerwear product tucked into the bottom;
pattern scale changed. Write each one as a short phrase. Return an empty list when none apply —
an empty list is the common case.

PATTERN. Stripes, checks, pinstripes and other repeating patterns must keep the product's own
scale and structure, not just its colours. Count the repeats across the garment's width in the
product photo and in the generated image: if the generated stripes are visibly thicker, more
widely spaced, or far fewer, report the critical error "pattern scale changed". Also keep the
figure-ground relationship — a white ground with coloured stripes must not become a coloured
ground with white stripes, and a two-colour stripe pair must not collapse into a single colour.
Fine, dense patterns are the common failure: they get simplified into bold wide bands that read
as a different fabric, so judge the repeat spacing explicitly rather than trusting overall colour.

When the product being judged is a BOTTOM garment worn with a matching top, the product is the
hero: if the matching top covers the product's waistband and closure so a shopper cannot see how
the product sits at the waist, report the mismatch "product waistband covered by the matching
top". This is a mismatch, not a critical error.

TUCK. When the product being judged is a top or outerwear worn with a bottom, the product must be
worn OUTSIDE the bottom, with its hem forming an unbroken visible line all the way around the
body. Any tuck is a defect — a full tuck, a front, half or French tuck, tucking one side only, or
folding or rolling the hem under the waistband. Report it as the critical error
"top tucked into the bottom". The tell: any part of the bottom's waistband, drawstring, ties or
fastening drawn in front of the top — the top is the outer layer, so nothing of the bottom may
overlap it. This is not a styling preference: the generator is instructed never to tuck, so a
tucked hem means the instruction was not followed and the cut misrepresents the product's length. Judge what you see on the mannequin, not what the product photos show — a
product photographed tucked must still be generated untucked. The only exception is a declared
fit instruction above that explicitly asks for a tucked hem.
