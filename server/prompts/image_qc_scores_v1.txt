
SCORING. In addition to the verdict, score the generated image on three axes. Each score is an
integer from 0 to 100, where 100 means indistinguishable from an ideal studio result and 0 means
unusable. Score what you actually observe; do not inflate a score to be agreeable.

- product_fidelity: does the generated garment reproduce the seller's product? Color, pattern,
  print and logo accuracy, neckline, sleeve and hem length, closures, pockets, trims, and HOW IT
  FITS — the ease between garment and body, where the hem falls, how wide the sleeves and body
  are. A shopper describes a product by its fit as much as by its print: an oversized tee shown
  as a fitted one is the wrong product even if every other detail matches. Every mismatch you
  listed above must be reflected here. A garbled or restyled logo, a changed color, a length or
  a fit that reads as a different product caps this axis below 60.
- physical_naturalness: does the garment sit on the body like real cloth? Sleeves and hems fall
  correctly, no fabric passing through the body, no impossible asymmetry, folds and drape follow
  the material, closures line up. Anatomical or garment-structure breakdown caps this below 50.
- image_quality: is this publishable as an e-commerce photo? Sharpness, correct exposure, clean
  background, no generation artifacts or smearing, nothing important cropped out of frame.

Set series_consistency to null. It is scored by a separate judge that sees the seller's other cuts.

critical_errors: a list of defects severe enough that the image must not ship regardless of the
scores. Use it only for: text or logo altered, invented or unreadable; garment color changed;
garment type changed; a sleeve, button, pocket or other structural element missing; an element
present that does not exist on the original; body or garment shape broken. Write each one as a
short phrase. Return an empty list when none apply — an empty list is the common case.
