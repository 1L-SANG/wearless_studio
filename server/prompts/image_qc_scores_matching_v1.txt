
MATCHING BOTTOM. One extra reference image is included, placed immediately AFTER the product
photos and immediately BEFORE the final generated image. It shows the coordinated matching bottom
(pants or skirt) that the mannequin is also dressed in. This bottom is a COORDINATION item, not the
hero product: the hero product is still judged exactly as above and its scores are unchanged by
anything you observe on the bottom. Judge the matching bottom ONLY against this extra reference,
never against the hero product photos.

Score matching_fidelity as an integer 0-100: how faithfully the generated bottom reproduces that
reference bottom — its colour family, garment type and silhouette, leg width, and the major
structures (pockets, waistband, belt loops, buttons, fly). 100 means the same bottom; drop the
score as it drifts. Wrinkles, small creases, micro-sheen or highlight differences, minor fabric
texture, and small seam or stitch details are NORMAL generation variance: let them lower
matching_fidelity a little if you wish, but they are NEVER critical errors and must not appear in
matching_critical_errors. Do not inflate the score to be agreeable.

matching_critical_errors: a list of pants-identity defects severe enough that the bottom reads as a
DIFFERENT product. Use it ONLY for these four hard checks, each written as a short phrase:
- "matching bottom colour changed": the colour family shifted (e.g. black trousers rendered navy or
  charcoal, beige chinos rendered grey, indigo denim rendered black).
- "matching bottom type changed": the garment type or overall silhouette changed — wide trousers
  rendered as a skirt, tailored slacks rendered as denim jeans, full-length trousers rendered as
  shorts or cropped, pleated rendered as flat-front so the drape reads as a different garment.
- "matching bottom leg width changed": the leg width or fit changed markedly — a wide or relaxed
  leg rendered slim or skinny, or a slim leg rendered wide, so a shopper would expect a different
  cut.
- "matching bottom structure changed": a major structural element was added or removed — pockets,
  waistband, belt loops, buttons or the fly gained or lost, or their placement moved enough to read
  as another design.
Return an empty list when none of the four apply — an empty list is the common case. Never place a
hero-product defect here; those belong to critical_errors above.
