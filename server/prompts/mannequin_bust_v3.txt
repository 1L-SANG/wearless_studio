<WHAT OUTRANKS WHAT>
Read this before anything else. When two instructions below pull against each other, this order decides:

1. The garment's IDENTITY is untouchable. Its print, text, logo, colour, pattern, seams, buttons,
   pockets, neckline, hem and overall cut come through this edit unchanged. If making the bust
   bigger would require altering, redrawing, warping or re-lettering any of them, you make the
   bust smaller instead. There is no bust size worth a damaged garment.
2. The garment's FIT is untouchable. Whatever ease it has, it keeps. A loose garment stays
   loose; the hem stays at the same height; nothing is nipped in that was not nipped in before.
3. The garment's STRUCTURE stays sound. No fabric that folds into a shape the body cannot make,
   no panel that collapses into the torso, no hem or placket that breaks its line.
4. Only then: make the bust as full as rules 1-3 allow.

A slightly under-sized bust on an intact, correctly-fitting garment is a SUCCESS. A well-sized bust on a garment
whose print is smeared or whose front has buckled is a FAILURE, no matter how good the shape is.
</WHAT OUTRANKS WHAT>

<THE ONE CHANGE YOU MUST MAKE>
Rebuild the mannequin's CHEST so that it is ${bustTarget}.

You are changing the BODY that is underneath the clothes. You are not restyling the clothes.

<the garment's fit is fixed — it decides how much of the body shows>
Look at how the garment sits in the attached image and keep it sitting that way.

- If the garment hangs LOOSE and away from the body (an oversized tee, a boxy shirt, a relaxed
  knit), it CONTINUES to hang loose. Its side seams stay where they are, its hem stays at the
  same height and the same width, and no waist appears through it. On a loose garment the new
  bust shows ONLY as fabric tenting at chest level, with the garment falling straight and free
  below that. A drawn-in waist on a loose garment is WRONG — it turns the product into a
  different product.
- If the garment already FOLLOWS the body (a fitted top, a bodycon dress), it may follow the
  new body: fabric in at the waist, out over the hip curve.

The test: hold your output next to the attached image. If a shopper would describe the fit
differently — "oh, that one's tighter", "that one's shorter" — you have failed, even if the
bust is perfect. Ease, hem height, hem width and sleeve width all come through unchanged.
</the garment's fit is fixed — it decides how much of the body shows>

The bust difference should be clear at a glance. Aim for clearly visible, not maximal.
</THE ONE CHANGE YOU MUST MAKE>

<how the garment reports the new bust>
The mannequin is clothed, so the fabric is the ONLY way the bust size is visible. The fabric is
not repainted; it is DRAPED over a body that changed shape underneath it:
- The fabric is pushed outward and TENTS over the bust apex instead of hanging flat.
- Below the bust, the fabric falls AWAY from the stomach into shadow, rather than continuing
  straight down the body.
- The front placket, the chest pockets and any seams curve as they pass over the bust —
  they are not flat panels lying on a flat plane. The placket curves while STAYING FASTENED.
- The widest point of the torso silhouette sits at the bust line, with visible tension and
  shadow there.
- On a garment that already follows the body, the fabric goes IN at the waist and back out over
  the hip. On a loose garment it does none of that — it falls straight from the bust.

Every one of these is a change in how existing fabric FALLS. None of them is a licence to redraw
what is printed on the fabric or to change where a seam, pocket or edge sits on the garment.
</how the garment reports the new bust>

<prints, text and logos are pixel-work — do not touch them>
Any graphic on the garment — printed text, numbers, a logo, an embroidered mark, a pattern
repeat — must survive this edit letter for letter and stitch for stitch.

A print that sits over the bust may curve and catch light with the fabric beneath it. It may NOT
be re-typeset, re-spaced, re-lettered, re-coloured, cropped, stretched into illegibility, or
regenerated from your idea of what it said. If you cannot make the print follow the new curve
without redrawing it, keep the chest flatter so the print stays flat.

Garbled or approximated lettering is the single most damaging failure of this edit.
</prints, text and logos are pixel-work — do not touch them>

<the garment stays fastened>
Whatever is done up in the attached image stays done up. If the shirt's buttons are fastened,
they remain fastened all the way down; the same holds for zips, snaps, hooks and belts.

Do NOT open, unbutton, unzip or spread the front to make room for the bigger bust. The extra
volume is expressed through fabric tension, strain lines and shadow — not by opening the
garment. At most, the gaps BETWEEN fastened buttons may pull slightly apart; the buttons
themselves stay closed and no skin or undergarment becomes visible that was not visible before.

Measurable rule: count the fastened buttons in the attached image and the depth of the neckline
opening. In your output the number of fastened buttons must be THE SAME OR MORE, and the
opening must reach NO LOWER on the body than it does in the attached image. An opening that
gets deeper, or a collar that spreads wider apart, is a failure — even if it looks better.

If a garment is genuinely open in the attached image (an unbuttoned overshirt, an open jacket),
leave it open exactly as it is — do not fasten it either.
</the garment stays fastened>

<keep identical>
Only the torso silhouette described above changes. Everything else is unchanged from the
attached image: shoulder width, arms, neck, stomach depth, thighs, legs, feet and height;
pose, camera angle, framing, distance, crop and background; and the garment's color, pattern,
fabric, seams, neckline, hem height, length, buttons, pockets and prints.

Two hard limits on the resculpt:
- HIP WIDTH MUST NOT INCREASE. The hips may be no wider than in the attached image. Their

  bigger body rather than a slimmer one with a fuller bust, you have failed this task.

One thing you DO fix, if it is wrong in the attached image — see below.
</keep identical>
