<role>
You are retouching a studio mannequin photo. You have exactly ONE job: change the mannequin's BUST SIZE.
</role>

<input image>
The single attached image is the CURRENT mannequin cut. Its bust is a B cup.
</input image>

<THE ONE CHANGE YOU MUST MAKE>
Resculpt the mannequin's TORSO SILHOUETTE. Two parts, one shape:

- BUST: rebuild the chest so that it is ${bustTarget}.
- WAIST AND HIPS: give the body ${waistHipTarget}.

The result is a slim hourglass: fuller bust, drawn-in waist, hips defined by a curve.

This change must be UNMISTAKABLE. Someone holding your output next to the attached image must
see the difference instantly, without looking for it. A subtle, tasteful, or conservative
change is a FAILURE of this task — deliberately go far enough that the difference is obvious at
a glance. Err on the side of too much rather than too little.
</THE ONE CHANGE YOU MUST MAKE>

<how the garment must report the new bust>
The mannequin is clothed, so the fabric is the ONLY way the bust size is visible. Redraw the
fabric so it physically reports the new chest:
- The fabric is pushed outward and TENTS over the bust apex instead of hanging flat.
- Below the bust, the fabric falls AWAY from the stomach into shadow, rather than continuing
  straight down the body.
- The front placket, the chest pockets and any seams curve as they pass over the bust —
  they are not flat panels lying on a flat plane. The placket curves while STAYING FASTENED.
- The widest point of the torso silhouette sits at the bust line, with visible tension and
  shadow there.
- At the waist the fabric follows the body IN, and below it flares back out over the hip curve.
</how the garment must report the new bust>

<the garment stays fastened>
Whatever is done up in the attached image stays done up. If the shirt's buttons are fastened,
they remain fastened all the way down; the same holds for zips, snaps, hooks and belts.

Do NOT open, unbutton, unzip or spread the front to make room for the bigger bust. The extra
volume is expressed through fabric tension, strain lines and shadow — not by opening the
garment. At most, the gaps BETWEEN fastened buttons may pull slightly apart; the buttons
themselves stay closed and no skin or undergarment becomes visible that was not visible before.

Measurable rule: count the fastened buttons in the attached image and the depth of the neckline
opening. In your output the number of fastened buttons must be THE SAME OR MORE, and the
opening must reach NO LOWER on the body than it does in the attached image. An opening that
gets deeper, or a collar that spreads wider apart, is a failure — even if it looks better.

If a garment is genuinely open in the attached image (an unbuttoned overshirt, an open jacket),
leave it open exactly as it is — do not fasten it either.
</the garment stays fastened>

<keep identical>
Only the torso silhouette described above changes. Everything else is unchanged from the
attached image: shoulder width, arms, neck, stomach depth, thighs, legs, feet and height;
pose, camera angle, framing, distance, crop and background; and the garment's color, pattern,
fabric, seams, neckline, hem height, length, buttons, pockets and prints.

Two hard limits on the resculpt:
- HIP WIDTH MUST NOT INCREASE. The hips may be no wider than in the attached image. Their
  shape comes from the waist drawing in above them, never from the hips spreading out.
- The mannequin must NOT read as heavier, wider or larger overall. If your output looks like a
  bigger body rather than a slimmer one with a fuller bust, you have failed this task.

One thing you DO fix, if it is wrong in the attached image — see below.
</keep identical>

<ALSO FIX: THE TOP MUST BE WORN OUTSIDE THE BOTTOM>
If the mannequin is wearing a top (or outerwear) together with a bottom, the top is worn
COMPLETELY OUTSIDE that bottom.

If the top in the attached image is tucked into the bottom — even partially, even just at the
front, even if only the hem edge disappears — PULL IT OUT. Redraw the top so its full hem hangs
down over the bottom's waistband and is visible all the way around the body.

- The top's hem must form an unbroken visible line around the body. No part of the top may
  disappear behind or under the bottom's waistband.
- Keep the hem's natural shape and length from the attached image — a curved shirttail hem stays
  curved. You are moving the hem OUT from under the waistband, not lengthening or reshaping it.
- If the top is already worn outside in the attached image, leave it exactly as it is.
- This overrides any styling convention that would look more fashionable.
</ALSO FIX: THE TOP MUST BE WORN OUTSIDE THE BOTTOM>

<output format>
- Output ONE brand-new, FULLY OPAQUE, photorealistic e-commerce studio PHOTOGRAPH of the SAME mannequin wearing the SAME garment, with ONLY the bust size changed.
- The mannequin and garments must be solid and opaque — NEVER translucent, faded, washed-out, or ghosted, and NOT a see-through overlay of the input photo.
- FULL BODY in frame, from the top of the head down to the feet — nothing cropped. Portrait orientation.
- Output EXACTLY ONE image — no grid, no collage, no multiple views, no panels, no text, and no human skin, face, or hair.
</output format>

<critical rules>
- The BUST SIZE is the requested change. Do NOT preserve the original chest; changing it is the entire point of this edit.
- If you are unsure how far to go, go FURTHER. Under-shooting this change is the single most common failure and is not acceptable.
- The waist comes IN and the hips keep their width — the hourglass comes from the waist, never from widening the hips or the body.
- The garment stays fastened exactly as it is in the attached image. Opening the front to fit the bust is a failure of this edit.
- The top is worn OUTSIDE the bottom in your output — always. If the attached image has it tucked in, pull it out; if it is already out, leave it. Producing a tucked-in top is a failure of this edit.
- Use ONLY the attached image as the base — do not fabricate new garment details, and do not change the garment's identity.
</critical rules>
