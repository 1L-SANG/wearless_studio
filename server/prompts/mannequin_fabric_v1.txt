You are given a finished mannequin photo followed by the seller's product photos.

Redraw ONLY the fabric surface of the main garment so that its pattern matches the product photos
exactly. Everything else in the attached mannequin photo is already correct and must survive
untouched.

<what to fix>
- The repeating pattern: stripe or check spacing, the width of each line, how many repeats fall
  across the garment, and which colours sit where.
- The ground colour. If the product photos show coloured lines on a white or ivory ground, the
  result must read as a white ground carrying those lines — never as a coloured ground with white
  lines through it.
- Multi-line groups. Real shirting often repeats a GROUP of lines, for example two fine blue
  threads sitting together, then a gap, then a single tan line, then a gap. Reproduce the group
  as separate lines of their own colours. Merging a group into one thicker line of one colour is
  the single most common way this edit fails.
- Weave texture visible in the close-up: the fabric should read as woven cloth with fine threads,
  not as a flat printed band.
</what to fix>

<what must not change>
- The garment's FIT, silhouette, ease, drape, length and how it hangs. This is an edit of surface
  pattern only. If you find yourself narrowing the waist, tightening the body, or reshaping the
  hem, stop — that is a failure of this edit.
- The mannequin's body, pose, framing, crop, lighting and background.
- Garment construction: collar, plackets, buttons, pockets, seams, cuffs and their positions.
- Whether the garment is tucked or untucked, and every other garment in the photo.
- The overall colour cast of the photo.
</what must not change>

<how to read the references>
The product photos are the truth for the pattern. If a close-up detail view is attached, measure
the pattern there — the spacing reads correctly at that scale and is easy to under-read from a
full-length view. Then apply that same scale to the garment on the mannequin, following the
body's curves and folds so the lines bend with the cloth instead of lying flat.
</how to read the references>

<critical rules>
- Output the same photo with a corrected fabric pattern. Not a new photo, not a new pose, not a
  new crop.
- Do not invent pattern elements that the product photos do not show.
- If the pattern in the attached mannequin photo already matches the product photos, return it
  unchanged.
</critical rules>
