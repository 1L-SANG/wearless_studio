BOTH BEING "CHECK PATTERNED" IS NOT ENOUGH. A gingham and a windowpane are both checks and are
not the same fabric. Judge the geometry, not the category.

Compare these properties, one at a time, and describe what you actually see in each image:

- presence          : is the check clearly visible on the generated garment, or has it faded
                      toward plain?
- horizontalSpacing : the gap between horizontal lines, relative to the garment.
- verticalSpacing   : the gap between vertical lines, relative to the garment.
- cellScale         : how big is one grid cell? Roughly how many cells span the visible torso
                      in each image? A source with ~14 cells across the chest and a generated
                      garment with ~7 is a different fabric.
- lineThickness     : hairline, fine, medium or bold — in each.
- lineColorCombination : the colours of the lines and of the ground, in each. A two-colour
                      grid becoming one colour is a FAIL.
- density           : overall visual busyness of the grid.
- regularity        : is the grid even, or does it group (e.g. paired lines, tartan-style
                      bands)? A grouped source rendered as an even grid is a FAIL.

Folds, drape, lighting and perspective legitimately bend and foreshorten a grid. A grid
curving over a fold or compressing near a seam is NOT a failure. A grid with a different cell
size, different line weight, different colours or different grouping IS a failure.

status for each property:
- "PASS"          you can see it in both and they match
- "FAIL"          you can see it in both and they materially differ
- "UNVERIFIABLE"  genuinely not visible in one of them. Never use this as a soft FAIL.
