PRODUCT IMPACT ASSESSMENT (scored mannequin requests only).
Return product_risks with one object per axis: logo_graphic, color, construction,
pattern, material, fit. Each object has severity and evidence. Use severity none,
minor, major, critical, or uncertain. Evidence must describe the visible source
feature and the generated result, including when no defect is found. Inspect every
axis; do not return an empty or partial report. This rubric refines the severity
of the mismatches and critical_errors above and takes precedence for severity.

none: the product is correctly represented. Natural folds, contact shadows, normal
studio highlights and small viewpoint changes are not product defects.
minor: a small observable difference that would not change a customer's purchase
decision or expectation of the product. Do not add multiple minor issues together
to turn them into a major or critical issue.
major: a clear, source-proven difference affecting a customer's expectation of the
garment, even if its broad identity is recognizable. Correction is needed.
critical: source-proven loss or alteration of a defining product feature, such as a
missing or garbled visible logo, changed color family, garment type, or major pocket
or closure structure. Other correct features never offset a critical defect.
uncertain: visible evidence suggests a potentially important mismatch but the
comparison cannot be resolved. An unseen back in a front mannequin view, an internal
label hidden by normal wear, or another normally out-of-view region is outside the
inspection scope, not an uncertain defect. If visible applicable features match,
record none and describe that visible match. Uploaded back references explain the
product; they do not require the front output to show the back. A feature specifically
required to remain visible but incorrectly covered is still an observable defect.

Axis guidance:
- logo_graphic: exterior logos, text, embroidery and distinctive graphics. Missing,
  invented or garbled visible marks are critical; tiny rendering variation with
  intact text and design can be minor. Apply the mirrored-source rule above. If the
  original has no exterior mark and none was generated, record none with that evidence.
- color: separate normal light/shadow from actual pigment/color-family changes.
  A slight neutral lighting cast may be none/minor; a visibly different product color
  is critical. Do not infer a global product color from a local button/label close-up.
- construction: neckline, pockets, seams, trims, closure type and exterior hardware.
  Missing/added defining construction is critical; small stitch differences can be
  minor. Never infer absence from a hidden source region or invent exposed hardware
  based on an internal closure photograph.
- pattern: preserve motif, scale, spacing, direction and figure/ground relationship.
  Small perspective variation is normal; a clear altered repeat is major, and an
  entirely different motif or merged defining stripe colors is critical.
- material: preserve source-evidenced texture, finish, thickness and native sheerness.
  Natural wrinkles or slight micro-sheen are not major issues. A clearly changed
  weave, knit or opacity affecting product identity is major/critical.
- fit: compare against seller-declared fit targets when present, otherwise the photographed fit.
  Legitimate seller-declared changes are correct. A materially wrong silhouette,
  hem position, or prohibited tuck is major/critical; normal drape is not a defect.

Assess the MAIN PRODUCT only in product_risks. Use the separate matching fields for
the coordination item if provided. Body breakdown and unusable image artifacts still
belong in the existing technical scores and critical_errors. Keep critical_errors
consistent with this rubric; it contains only actual critical defects, not minor
differences, uncertainty, or a count of concerns. Put a concise source-grounded repair
instruction for repairable major/critical findings in correctionPrompt and set verdict=retry.

Pattern and material findings are review-only for mannequin generation. Report their
severity and evidence in product_risks so they remain visible to comparison and review,
but do not ask to redraw pattern, texture, weave, finish or sheen in correctionPrompt.
Do not copy a pattern/material-only finding into critical_errors, mismatches or a retry
verdict, because those fields authorize another paid image attempt. Keep its structured
severity even when it is critical so the server marks the first cut for review.
When another confirmed structural, fit, color, logo or text defect also needs correction,
target only that defect and explicitly preserve the existing surface appearance.

REPAIR DIRECTION QUALITY. For each confirmed major/critical issue, specify the affected
garment role and visible region, the source observation, the actual output difference,
and the smallest corrective action with protected neighboring features. Use garment
landmarks such as placket, seam junction, cuff edge and hem, not invented body measurements.
Do not merely say "improve fidelity", "restore texture" or "fix pattern". Keep the complete
correctionPrompt within 1600 characters, prioritizing confirmed defining errors.

For a pattern, inspect uninterrupted body and sleeve panels for line continuity, spacing,
line widths and crossings. Lines may bend at real folds or end at actual seams/occlusions;
unexplained gaps on visible continuous cloth are not cured just because a grid is present.
For material, compare visible stitch/loop/weave structure and surface response, not just
the broad garment class. Replacing visible fine rows with diffuse fuzzy or directional
pile-like shading can change identity even if it still looks like a sweatshirt or trousers.
Do not infer fiber composition, and account for lighting and unequal pixel scale.
For readable lettering, transcribe source and generated text separately and identify the
specific differing characters. If a character cannot actually be resolved, mark uncertainty
rather than inventing a confident misspelling and authorizing a repair from it.
